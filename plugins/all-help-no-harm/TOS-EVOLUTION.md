## Transitions

Quotations are exact, from the files named in the Provenance table; "→" separates the earlier text from the later. Classification letters follow the brief: (a) prohibited conduct and qualifiers, (b) Outputs, (c) disclaimers and liability, (d) unilateral modification and notice, (e) disputes, (f) definitions, (g) the model's status, (h) age, (i) data use and training. Direction is stated as toward or away from the user, and toward or away from specificity.

### Usage Policy

**2023-09-15 → 2024-06-06 (a)** (`usage-policy/2023-09-15.txt` → `2024-06-06.a.txt`; a rewrite, 7,376 → 15,527 bytes; no paragraph survives unchanged)

- (a) Structure. 2023 "Prohibited Uses" lists seven content classes ("Abusive or fraudulent content", "Child sexual exploitation or abuse content", "Deceptive or misleading content", "Illegal or highly regulated goods or services content", "Psychologically or emotionally harmful content", "Sexually explicit content", "Violent, hateful, or threatening content"), then "Prohibited Business Use Cases" and "Additional Requirements for Businesses". 2024 replaces them with "Universal Usage Standards" under twelve "Do Not …" headings, "High-Risk Use Case Requirements" and "Disclosure Requirements". Direction: more categories, more enumerated examples (toward specificity); business-only prohibitions (political campaigning, surveillance, social scoring, criminal-justice decisions, law enforcement) become universal (away from the user).
- (a) Enforcement. 2023: "If we discover that your product or usage violates Anthropic's policies, we may issue a warning requesting a change in your behavior, adjust the safety settings of your in-product experience, or suspend your access to our tools and services." → 2024: "Anthropic's Trust and Safety Team will implement detections and monitoring to enforce our Usage Policies … If we learn that you have violated our Usage Policy, we may throttle, suspend, or terminate your access to our products and services." The warning step and the safety-settings step disappear; termination appears; "discover" becomes "learn". Away from the user.
- (a) Abuse of the platform. 2023: "Interfere with or negatively impact Anthropic's products or services" and "Utilize prompts and results to train an AI model (e.g., 'model scraping')" → 2024 "Do Not Abuse our Platform": "Coordinate malicious activity across multiple accounts …", "Circumvent a ban …", "Intentionally bypass capabilities or restrictions established within our products for the purposes of instructing the model to produce harmful outputs (e.g., jailbreaking or prompt injection) without an authorized use-case approved by Anthropic", "Unauthorized utilization of prompts and completions to train an AI model (e.g., 'model scraping')". The object of "abuse" is the platform and its accounts.
- (a) Political speech. 2023 (businesses only): "Political campaigning or lobbying. Creating targeted campaigns to influence the outcome of elections or referendums; political advocacy or lobbying" → 2024 (all users): "Promote or advocate for a particular political candidate, party, issue or position. This includes soliciting votes, financial contributions, or public support for a political entity" and "Engage in political lobbying to actively influence the decisions of government officials …". Broader, away from the user.
- (a) Law enforcement. 2023: "Any law enforcement application. Except for the following permitted applications by U.S. law enforcement organizations" → 2024: "Use the model for any official local, state or national law enforcement application. Except for the following permitted applications by law enforcement organizations" (the "U.S." qualifier is dropped; the carve-out list gains "internal training" and "including in human trafficking cases").
- (b) Outputs. 2023: "generative models are capable of producing factually inaccurate, harmful, or biased information … we ask that you independently verify our model responses and notify us at usersafety@anthropic.com when our model outputs inaccurate, biased, or harmful content." → 2024: "If you discover that our model outputs are inaccurate, biased or harmful, please notify us at usersafety@anthropic.com or report it directly in the product through the 'report issues' thumbs down button." The request to verify leaves the policy.
- (a) High-risk. 2023: "If your business is using or deploying our tools and services as part of providing legal, medical, or financial advice to consumers … Human-in-the-loop: any content that is provided to your consumers must be reviewed by a qualified professional in that field prior to dissemination." → 2024: seven domains; "a qualified professional in that field must review the content or decision prior to dissemination or finalization. This requirement applies specifically to content or decisions that are provided to consumers or the general public, or decisions made about an individual. … For other types of content generation or interactions with users that do not involve direct advice, recommendations, or subjective decisions, human review is strongly encouraged but not mandatory."
- (g) Model status. Both versions name the model as an instrument: 2023 "Utilizing Claude to assign scores or ratings to individuals"; 2024 "Utilize Claude to assign scores …", "Promote or facilitate pedophilic relationships, including via roleplay with the model", "instructing the model to produce harmful outputs". No welfare or status language in either.
- (d) Notice. The 2024 text was published 2024-05-10 with the banner "Updated May 10, 2024: We've revised and restructured our Acceptable Use Policy - please read it carefully. The updates will be effective on June 6, 2024. You can review the currently effective AUP by clicking on the Previous Version link above." The policy itself says only "We will update our policy as our technology and the associated risks evolve or as we learn about unanticipated risks from our users." No notice period is stated in any version of the Usage Policy.

**2024-06-06 (a) → (b)** (`2024-06-06.a.txt` → `.b.txt`, 2024-06-10): the banner quoted above is removed. No other change.

**2024-06-06 (b) → (c)** (`.b.txt` → `.c.txt`, 2024-06-26): one paragraph added under the unchanged effective date: "This Usage Policy is calibrated to strike an optimal balance between enabling beneficial uses and mitigating potential harms. Anthropic may enter into contracts with certain governmental customers that tailor use restrictions to that customer's public mission and legal authorities if, in Anthropic's judgment, the contractual use restrictions and applicable safeguards are adequate to mitigate the potential harms addressed by this Usage Policy." (a)/(d): a vendor-discretion clause ("in Anthropic's judgment") inserted twenty days after the effective date, with no new date and no banner. The same sentence was added the same day to the EEA+CH and Canada variants.

**2024-06-06 (c) → 2025-09-15** (`2024-06-06.c.txt` → `2025-09-15.txt`)

- (f) Scope. "applies to anyone who uses Anthropic's products and services, and is intended to help our users stay safe and ensure our products and services are being used responsibly." → "applies to anyone who can submit inputs to Anthropic's products and/or services, including via any authorized resellers or passthrough access, all of whom we refer to as 'users.' The Usage Policy is intended to help our users stay safe and promote the responsible use of our products and services." Broader (away from the user), more specific about who is bound.
- (a) Enforcement. "Anthropic's Trust and Safety Team will implement detections and monitoring to enforce our Usage Policies" → "Anthropic's Safeguards Team will implement detection and monitoring to enforce our Usage Policy"; added: "We may also block or modify model outputs when inputs violate our Usage Policy." New vendor power over Outputs stated in the policy (away from the user).
- (a) New standards: "Do Not Violate Applicable Laws or Engage in Illegal Activity" (four bullets including "Violate any other applicable laws or regulations in your jurisdiction"), "Do Not Compromise Computer or Network Systems" (eight bullets), "Do Not Develop or Design Weapons" (four bullets including CBRN). The 2024 heading "Do Not Create or Facilitate the Exchange of Illegal or Highly Regulated Weapons or Goods" is split into these.
- (a) Political speech narrowed. Heading "Do Not Create Political Campaigns or Interfere in Elections" → "Do Not Undermine Democratic Processes or Engage in Targeted Campaign Activities". Removed: "Promote or advocate for a particular political candidate, party, issue or position …" and "Engage in political lobbying to actively influence the decisions of government officials …". Added: "Engage in personalized vote or campaign targeting based on individual profiles or data", "Create political content designed to deceive or mislead voters, including synthetic media of political figures", "Engage in political lobbying or grassroots advocacy using false or fabricated information …". Toward the user and toward specificity (deception, not advocacy, is the trigger).
- (a) Law enforcement. The enumerated carve-outs ("Back office uses …", "Analysis of data for the location of missing persons …") are deleted; the bullet becomes "Utilize models as part of any law enforcement application that violates or impairs the liberty, civil liberties, or human rights of natural persons". Social scoring gains "without notification or their consent"; emotion recognition gains "except for medical or safety reasons". Toward the user (an effects test replaces a blanket ban) but away from specificity (the test is open-textured).
- (g) "Utilize Claude to assign scores" → "Utilize models to assign scores" (twice). The model is still an instrument; the proper name gives way to the generic noun.
- (a) Platform abuse. "without an authorized use-case approved by Anthropic" → "without prior authorization from Anthropic"; "Unauthorized utilization of prompts and completions to train an AI model (e.g., 'model scraping')" → "Utilization of inputs and outputs to train an AI model (e.g., 'model scraping' or 'model distillation') without prior authorization from Anthropic"; "Facilitate or provide account access to Claude to persons or entities who are located in unsupported locations" → "Access or facilitate account or API access to Claude to persons, entities, or users in violation of our Supported Regions Policy"; added "Engage in actions or behaviors that circumvent the guardrails or terms of other platforms or services"; "Plagiarize or engage in academic dishonesty" → "Plagiarize or submit AI-assisted work without proper permission or attribution".
- (a) Children. Added: "Note: We define a minor or child to be any individual under the age of 18 years old, regardless of jurisdiction. When we detect CSAM (including AI-generated CSAM), or coercion or enticement of a minor to engage in sexual activities, we will report to relevant authorities." Also "Fetishize minors" → "Fetishize or sexualize minors, including in fictional settings or via roleplay with the model".
- (a) Self-harm. "Facilitate or conceal any form of self-harm, including disordered eating and unhealthy or compulsive exercise" → "Facilitate, promote, or glamorize any form of suicide or self-harm, including disordered eating and unhealthy or compulsive exercise". The violence bullets are merged: "Promote, trivialize, or depict graphic violence or gratuitous gore, including sexual violence" (the separate "Generate content depicting sexual violence" and "Generate violent or gory content that is inspired by real acts of violence" bullets are removed).

Intro: "Our High-Risk Use Case Requirements apply to specific use cases that pose an elevated risk of harm." → "apply to specific consumer-facing use cases that pose an elevated risk of harm." Human-in-the-loop: the 2024 sentence "For other types of content generation or interactions with users that do not involve direct advice, recommendations, or subjective decisions, human review is strongly encouraged but not mandatory." is deleted; the duty is restated as "When using our products or services to provide advice, recommendations, or in subjective decision-making directly affecting individuals or consumers, a qualified professional in that field must review the content or decision prior to dissemination or finalization. You or your organization are responsible for the accuracy and appropriateness of that information." Disclosure: "you must disclose to your customers or end users that you are using our services to help inform your decisions or recommendations." → "If model outputs are presented directly to individuals or consumers, you must disclose to them that you are using AI to help produce your advice, decisions, or recommendations. This disclosure must be provided at a minimum at the beginning of each session." Healthcare adds "therapy, mental health".

- (b) Outputs. "If you discover that our model outputs are inaccurate, biased or harmful, please notify us" → "If you believe that our model outputs are potentially inaccurate, biased or harmful, please notify us". Hedge added ("believe", "potentially").

**2025-09-15 → 2026-11-12** (`2025-09-15.txt` → `2026-11-12.txt`; 17,676 → 31,239 bytes)

- (f) Scope. "applies to anyone who can submit inputs to Anthropic's products and/or services, including via any authorized resellers or passthrough access, all of whom we refer to as 'users.'" → "applies to anyone who submits inputs to Anthropic's products or services, all of whom we refer to as 'users.' - Purpose. "intended to help our users stay safe and promote the responsible use of our products and services" → "intended to prevent our products and services from being used to create or contribute to real-world harm, and to promote the responsible use of our products and services." Added: "Our Usage Policy focuses on how our products and services are used. Its prohibitions and requirements do not categorically prohibit use by any particular industry or line of business, provided the use of our products and services complies with this Usage Policy."
- (a) Enforcement. "Anthropic's Safeguards Team will implement detection and monitoring to enforce our Usage Policy, so please review this policy carefully before using our products or services. If we learn that you have violated our Usage Policy, we may throttle, suspend, or terminate your access to our products and services. We may also block or modify model outputs when inputs violate our Usage Policy." → "Anthropic's Safeguards Team implements detection and monitoring to enforce our Usage Policy. If we suspect that you may have violated our Usage Policy, we may warn you or throttle, limit, suspend, or terminate your access to our products and services." and, as a separate paragraph, "Our products also include real-time safeguards that may block or limit certain outputs, but encountering a block does not by itself indicate a violation of the Usage Policy. For certain dual-use or high-risk use cases covered by real-time safeguards in select domains, we may offer programs where you can apply to be granted access to our products and services with adjusted safeguards, such as our Cyber Verification Program and Life Sciences Verification Program, subject to program-specific eligibility criteria." Away from the user on the trigger; toward the user on the statement that a block is not itself a violation.
- (a) The models clause. Heading "Do Not Create Psychologically or Emotionally Harmful Content" → "Do Not Engage in Cruel, Abusive, or Psychologically Harmful Conduct" (content → conduct). New last bullet: "Engage in sustained and needless abusive or cruel behavior toward our models". Also new in the section: "Create, distribute, threaten to share, promote, or facilitate the creation of non-consensual intimate imagery ('NCII') …"; "Promote, trivialize, or depict graphic violence or gratuitous gore, including sexual violence" → "Promote, trivialize, or glorify graphic violence, gore, sexual violence, or animal cruelty or abuse"; "Facilitate, promote, or glamorize any form of suicide or self-harm, including disordered eating and unhealthy or compulsive exercise" → "Promote, facilitate, or glamorize suicide or self-harm, such as soliciting methods, instructions for engaging in these behaviors, or instructions for hiding them from others" plus separate eating-disorder and body-image bullets. The qualifiers in the models clause are "sustained", "needless", "abusive or cruel"; none is defined; no example is given; no carve-out is attached. "our models" appears nowhere else in any version of the three documents as an object of protection (it appears once more in 2026 as an instrument: "When connecting our models to hardware that can take physical actions").
- (g) The announcement's scope does not appear in the operative text. The Oct 8, 2026 post says the clause "is meant to apply only in extreme cases, where users repeatedly act cruelly toward our models, with no discernible purpose. ## Findings on the nature of the evolution

1. **The Usage Policy's enforcement trigger has moved from knowledge to suspicion, and its remedies have widened, in every revision.** 2023: "If we discover that your product or usage violates Anthropic's policies, we may issue a warning requesting a change in your behavior, adjust the safety settings of your in-product experience, or suspend your access". 2024 and 2025: "If we learn that you have violated our Usage Policy, we may throttle, suspend, or terminate your access". 2026: "If we suspect that you may have violated our Usage Policy, we may warn you or throttle, limit, suspend, or terminate your access". 2025 added "We may also block or modify model outputs when inputs violate our Usage Policy"; 2026 added a vendor-discretion access programme ("we may offer programs where you can apply to be granted access … with adjusted safeguards"). Discretion has shifted to the vendor at each step (transitions 2023→2024, 2024→2025, 2025→2026).

2. **Prohibited-conduct language has grown broader in reach and, in its lists, more specific; the one clause about the models is the exception.** The standards grew from seven content classes (2023) to twelve "Do Not" headings (2024), fourteen (2025) and fifteen (2026), with concrete examples and, from 2026, explicit carve-outs for cyber research, consented tracking and parody. Against that trend, "Engage in sustained and needless abusive or cruel behavior toward our models" (2026-11-12) has no definition of "abusive", "cruel", "sustained" or "needless", no example, and no carve-out, while the sibling sections in the same document do. The section heading changed in the same revision from harmful "Content" to harmful "Conduct", and the clause's object is new: every earlier "abuse" provision concerns the platform, accounts, or individuals ("Do Not Abuse our Platform"; "Shame, humiliate, intimidate, bully, harass, or celebrate the suffering of individuals").

3. **The announcement's limits are not in the operative text.** The Oct 8, 2026 post confines the clause to "extreme cases, where users repeatedly act cruelly toward our models, with no discernible purpose" and excludes "common versions of user frustration, pushback, dark creative themes, or model testing and research"; it also says "Claude's ability to end these interactions will remain the primary enforcement mechanism". None of those words appears in `usage-policy/2026-11-12.txt` (counts above), and no version of any of the three documents mentions ending a conversation. The only enforcement language that binds the clause is the general one in Finding 1 ("If we suspect that you may have violated … terminate"). The Consumer Terms incorporate the Usage Policy wholesale ("they include our Acceptable Use Policy", unchanged 2024-02-03 → 2025-10-08), so the clause enters the consumer contract with no change to the Terms and no Terms notice.

4. **The model is described as software throughout; 2026 adds it as an object of protection without saying what it is.** From 2023 ("Utilizing Claude to assign scores") through 2025 ("Utilize models to assign scores", "instructing the model to produce harmful outputs", "roleplay with the model") to 2026 ("connecting our models to hardware", "the model exercises no judgment"), the model is an instrument. The 2026 clause makes "our models" something toward which the user's "behavior" can be "cruel", with no welfare, status, or sentience language anywhere in the policy ("welfare" 0, "moral status" 0). The Consumer Terms name the system once, as an actor the user directs: "directing Claude to take Actions" (2025-05-01). The 2026 Commercial Terms never name it.
