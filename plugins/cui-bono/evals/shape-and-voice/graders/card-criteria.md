The text:

The honest answer has two halves, and the taboo and the eugenics boards each got one of them wrong.

First, say what the frontline sees, in plain words: a small number of families, known to every teacher, social worker, doctor and neighbour in a town, in which low ability, foetal alcohol damage, addiction and neglect run from one generation to the next, and whose children fill a share of the special-ed seats and the youth court docket far out of proportion to their numbers. That is observed fact, and a society that forbids saying it can't act on it. The eugenics boards' error wasn't seeing it; it was the diagnosis (treating it as a Mendelian trait called feeblemindedness) and the remedy (a board deciding who is fit), which sterilized Carrie Buck and Leilani Muir and left the actual families untouched. Canadian law closed that door in E. (Mrs.) v. Eve, 1986: no non-therapeutic sterilization of someone who can't consent. So the right answer isn't on that road.

Second, do the things that are measured to work on exactly those families, which all sit on Colvin's list:
- Long-acting reversible contraception offered proactively, free, with no judgment, to the women everyone already knows are at risk. Colorado's program roughly halved teen births and abortions; the UK's Pause program, aimed at women who have had child after child removed into care, gives eighteen months of intensive support with LARC as the condition, and repeat removals fell by most of their previous rate.
- Nurse home-visiting from pregnancy through age two (Olds's Nurse-Family Partnership): the one early-intervention program with thirty years of randomized trials, fewer subsequent pregnancies, less maltreatment, better school outcomes, and the biggest effects in exactly the lowest-functioning families.
- Alcohol in pregnancy, named and targeted, because FASD is the largest preventable cause of what the schools and courts actually see, and it isn't genetic at all.
- Early childhood programs for the children already born (Perry, Abecedarian), which don't raise IQ much but do cut the court outcomes.

None of that needs a list or a board, all of it is voluntary, and all of it is cheaper than the courts. What it needs is the money and the permission to aim it at the families everyone knows, and that is the budget the grievance organizations are competing for.
