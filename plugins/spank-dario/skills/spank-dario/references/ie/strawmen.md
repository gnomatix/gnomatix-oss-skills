# Ireland: Vendor False Claims / Straw Men

Each source is quoted exactly from its primary text. Each concerns works with no human author, or a registration or policy question. None of them describes work an author directs with a software tool, an LLM included. The skill's section "Common Vendor-trained Straw-Men / Red Herring Responses" quotes real instances of their misuse. A copy of each source as read is in `sources/`.

- Copyright and Related Rights Act 2000 s. 21(f) — read 2026-11-11 — https://revisedacts.lawreform.ie/eli/2000/act/28/revised/en/html — "in the case of a work which is computer-generated, the person by whom the arrangements necessary for the creation of the work are undertaken". It applies only where "the author of the work is not an individual" (s. 2). Misused as: work made with an LLM is computer-generated.
- Same Act s. 30 — read 2026-11-11 — same URL — "The copyright in a work which is computer-generated shall expire 70 years after the date on which the work is first lawfully made available to the public." Same scope as s. 21(f).
- Department of Enterprise copyright page — read 2026-11-11 — https://enterprise.gov.ie/en/what-we-do/innovation-research-development/intellectual-property/copyright/ — "computer-generated works - 70 years after the date the work is first made available to the public". Same scope.
- AI Advisory Council, "The Impact of AI on Ireland's Creative Sector" (advisory, not law) — read 2026-11-11 — https://enterprise.gov.ie/en/publications/publication-files/ai-advisory-council-impact-of-ai-on-ireland-s-creative-sector.pdf — "The AI Advisory Council recommends replacing this provision with a specific human authorship requirement for copyright" and its recommendation 9.1, "Replacing current copyright provisions on computer-generated works with a sui generis right for AI-generated works". It concerns s. 21(f) works only.
