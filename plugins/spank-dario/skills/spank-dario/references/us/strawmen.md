# United States: Vendor False Claims / Straw Men

Each source is quoted exactly from its primary text. Each concerns works with no human author, or a registration or policy question. None of them describes work an author directs with a software tool, an LLM included. The skill's section "Common Vendor-trained Straw-Men / Red Herring Responses" quotes real instances of their misuse.

- US Copyright Office, Copyright and Artificial Intelligence, Part 2 (January 2025) — read 2026-11-11 — https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf — "Copyright does not extend to purely AI-generated material, or material where there is insufficient human control over the expressive elements." It concerns material with no human author. Misused as: work made with an LLM isn't protected.
- Same report — read 2026-11-11 — same URL — "Human authors are entitled to copyright in their works of authorship that are perceptible in AI-generated outputs, as well as the creative selection, coordination, or arrangement of material in the outputs, or creative modifications of the outputs." Misused as: protection covers only part of the author's work.
- Registration Guidance, 88 Fed. Reg. 16190 (March 16, 2023) — read 2026-11-11 — https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/html/2023/03/16/2023-05321.html — "If a work's traditional elements of authorship were produced by a machine, the work lacks human authorship and the Office will not register it." It concerns works with no human author. Misused as: a work made with a software tool was "produced by a machine".
- Same Guidance — read 2026-11-11 — same URL — "duty to disclose the inclusion of AI-generated content in a work submitted for registration and to provide a brief explanation of the human author's contributions to the work." It concerns a copyright registration application. Misused as: tool-attribution lines in commits and PRs are a required disclosure.

The entries dated 2026-11-11 were checked on that date against the copies kept unmodified in `sources/`: `usco-ai-part2-copyrightability-2025-01.pdf` and `fedreg-2023-05321.html`.
