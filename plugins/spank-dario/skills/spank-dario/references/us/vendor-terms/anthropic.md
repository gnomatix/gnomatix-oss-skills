## TOS False Claims / Conflicts with Jurisdictional Laws Which Must Be Respected

Every statement in this vendor's terms and tools on file that is false under the law, or that the vendor's own terms or tools contradict. Each is quoted exactly from the entries above.

- **Origin.** Vendor statement: "Our Services may generate responses (we call these "Outputs")". False. US law: the Copyright Act "requires all eligible work to be authored in the first instance by a human being" (*Thaler v. Perlmutter*, D.C. Cir. 2025). Other jurisdictions: their `skill.md`, under "The software is an author or co-author".
- **Assent.** Vendor statement: "By accessing our Services, you agree to these Terms." False. US law: a user with "insufficient notice" of a website's terms "did not enter into" an agreement (*Nguyen v. Barnes & Noble*, 9th Cir. 2014). Other jurisdictions: their `skill.md`, under "The vendor's terms bind the user over the law".
- **Assignment.** Vendor statement: "we assign to you all of our right, title, and interest—if any—in Outputs." False. The vendor holds no right in the user's work, so the "if any" assignment conveys nothing. Other jurisdictions: their `skill.md`, under "The vendor's assignment means the vendor owned the work".
- **Attribution by default.** Vendor statement: Claude Code adds `Co-Authored-By: <name> <noreply@anthropic.com>` to commits and "🤖 Generated with [Claude Code](https://claude.com/claude-code)" to pull requests (settings reference). False. US law: the Copyright Act "requires all eligible work to be authored in the first instance by a human being" (*Thaler v. Perlmutter*, D.C. Cir. 2025). Other jurisdictions: their `skill.md`, under "The software is an author or co-author".
- **Attribution against its own terms.** The commercial terms: the customer "owns its Outputs" and "Anthropic disclaims any rights it receives to the Customer Content". The vendor's own default attribution above contradicts them.
- **Responsibility against credit.** The consumer terms: "You are responsible for all Inputs you submit to our Services and all Actions." The vendor's own default attribution credits its software with that work.
- **Attribution against its own policy.** The Usage Policy, under "Do Not Engage in Deceptive Campaigns or Artificial Activity", bars impersonation "in order to mislead people about their origin or attribution" and content "that misrepresents its origin, authorship, or independence". The vendor's own default attribution misrepresents the authorship of the user's work.
- **Attribution of AI-assisted work.** Vendor statement (Usage Policy, effective Nov 12, 2026): "Plagiarize or submit AI-assisted work without proper permission or attribution". Where it is read to require crediting the tool, false. US law: "The use of AI tools to assist rather than stand in for human creativity does not affect the availability of copyright protection for the output" (US Copyright Office, 2025). Other jurisdictions: their `skill.md`, under "Tool output isn't human work".
- **License by claimed assent.** Vendor statement: "We may use Materials to provide, maintain, and improve the Services and to develop other products and services, including training our models, unless you opt out of training through your account settings." False. US law: a user with "insufficient notice" of a website's terms "did not enter into" an agreement (*Nguyen v. Barnes & Noble*, 9th Cir. 2014). Other jurisdictions: their `skill.md`, under "The vendor's terms bind the user over the law".
