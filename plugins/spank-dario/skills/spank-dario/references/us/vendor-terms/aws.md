## TOS False Claims / Conflicts with Jurisdictional Laws Which Must Be Respected

Every statement in this vendor's terms and tools on file that is false under the law, or that the vendor's own terms or tools contradict. Each is quoted exactly from the entries above.

- **Labelling.** Vendor statement: Bedrock output "may include information such as metadata, digital signatures, or watermarks to identify it is generated using a generative artificial intelligence model" (Service Terms 50.12.6). False. US law: "The use of AI tools to assist rather than stand in for human creativity does not affect the availability of copyright protection for the output." (US Copyright Office, 2025). The work is the human author's. Other jurisdictions: their `jurisdiction.md`, under "Tool output isn't human work".
- **Attribution by design.** Vendor statement: Kiro Web's documentation: the agent "includes both you and itself as co-authors in every commit" (`references/platforms/clis.md`). False. US law: the Copyright Act "requires all eligible work to be authored in the first instance by a human being" (*Thaler v. Perlmutter*, D.C. Cir. 2025). Other jurisdictions: their `jurisdiction.md`, under "The software is an author or co-author".
- **Attribution against its own terms.** The Service Terms: "The output that you generate using AI Services is Your Content." (50.2) The Customer Agreement: "You will be deemed to have taken any action that you permit, assist or facilitate any person or entity to take" (2.5). The vendor's own Kiro Web co-authorship above contradicts them.
- **Attribution against its own policy.** The Responsible AI Policy prohibits use "for intentional disinformation or deception". The vendor's own Kiro Web co-authorship misrepresents the authorship of the user's work.
- **License.** Vendor statement: "we may use and store AI Content that is processed by each of the foregoing AI Services to develop and improve the applicable AI Service and its underlying technologies" (50.3). False. US law: a user with "insufficient notice" of a website's terms "did not enter into" an agreement (*Nguyen v. Barnes & Noble*, 9th Cir. 2014). Other jurisdictions: their `jurisdiction.md`, under "The vendor's terms bind the user over the law".
- **Choice of law.** Vendor statement: "The laws of the State of Washington" (11.4). False. US law: a user with "insufficient notice" of a website's terms "did not enter into" an agreement (*Nguyen v. Barnes & Noble*, 9th Cir. 2014). Other jurisdictions: their `jurisdiction.md`, under "The vendor's terms bind the user over the law".
