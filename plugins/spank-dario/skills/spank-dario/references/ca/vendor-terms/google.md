## TOS False Claims / Conflicts with Jurisdictional Laws Which Must Be Respected

Every statement in this vendor's terms and tools on file that is false under the law, or that the vendor's own terms or tools contradict. Each is quoted exactly from `../../us/vendor-terms/google.md`, which holds the same documents, with Canadian law stated for each.

- **Origin.** Vendor statement: listed as deceptive: "providing services that appear to originate from you (or someone else) when they actually originate from us". False. No Canadian provision on file for this point; the Berne Convention: the author may claim authorship "independently of the author's economic rights, and even after the transfer of the said rights" (art. 6bis(1)). Other jurisdictions: their `skill.md`, under "Tool output isn't human work".
- **Human work.** Vendor statement: "misleading others into thinking that generative AI content was created by a human" and "claiming it was created solely by a human, in order to deceive". False. No Canadian provision on file for this point; the Berne Convention: the author may claim authorship "independently of the author's economic rights, and even after the transfer of the said rights" (art. 6bis(1)). Other jurisdictions: their `skill.md`, under "Tool output isn't human work".
- **Rights over the same content.** Vendor statement: "You acknowledge that Google may generate the same or similar content for others and that we reserve all rights to do so." False. The vendor holds no right in the user's work, so the "if any" assignment conveys nothing. Other jurisdictions: their `skill.md`, under "The vendor's assignment means the vendor owned the work".
- **Responsibility against origin.** The Gemini API terms: "you are solely responsible for the actions and tasks performed by the service". Antigravity: "You are solely responsible for: (a) the actions and tasks performed by an AI Agent". The vendor's own claim that the work originates "from us" contradicts them.
- **Agentic actions.** Vendor statement: "Agentic AI Actions are not Generated Output". False. Canadian law: "It should be obvious to Air Canada that it is responsible for all the information on its website. It makes no difference whether the information comes from a static page or a chatbot." (*Moffatt v. Air Canada*, 2024 BCCRT 149, para. 27) Other jurisdictions: their `skill.md`, under "The software, not its vendor, answers for what it says".
- **License.** Vendor statement: Unpaid Gemini API: "Google uses the content you submit to the Services and any generated responses to provide, improve, and develop Google products and services". Antigravity: "We use Interactions to evaluate, develop, and improve Google and Alphabet research, products, services and machine learning technologies." False. Canadian law: "Moral rights may not be assigned but may be waived in whole or in part" (Copyright Act s. 14.1(2)). Other jurisdictions: their `skill.md`, under "The vendor's terms bind the user over the law".
- **Choice of law.** Vendor statement: "California law will govern these terms and all disputes". False. Canadian law: "Moral rights may not be assigned but may be waived in whole or in part" (Copyright Act s. 14.1(2)). Other jurisdictions: their `skill.md`, under "The vendor's terms bind the user over the law".
