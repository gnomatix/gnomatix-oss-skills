# spank-dario: Canada

This jurisdiction's part of the skill. Every quotation is from `ca/law.md`, where its source and read mark are given.

## What the law says

- **Moral rights can't be assigned.** "Moral rights may not be assigned but may be waived in whole or in part" (Copyright Act s. 14.1(2)).
- **Infringement.** An act or omission contrary to the author's moral rights, without the author's consent, is an infringement (s. 28.1).
- **Vendor terms yield.** Anthropic's consumer terms apply California law "except to the extent that Canadian federal or provincial law requires otherwise" (`ca/vendor-terms/anthropic.md`).

## Vendor Liability

- **Moral rights.** "the court may grant to the holder of those rights all remedies by way of injunction, damages, accounts, delivery up and otherwise that are or may be conferred by law for the infringement of a right" (s. 34(2)).
- **The vendor answers for its agent's statements.** In *Moffatt v. Air Canada*, 2024 BCCRT 149, BC's Civil Resolution Tribunal held Air Canada liable for negligent misrepresentation by its website chatbot.
  - "Here, given their commercial relationship as a service provider and consumer, I find Air Canada owed Mr. Moffatt a duty of care." (para. 26)
  - "It should be obvious to Air Canada that it is responsible for all the information on its website. It makes no difference whether the information comes from a static page or a chatbot." (para. 27)
  - "I find Air Canada did not take reasonable care to ensure its chatbot was accurate." Correct information elsewhere didn't cure it: Air Canada didn't explain "why customers should have to double-check information found in one part of its website on another part of its website." (para. 28)
  - Its terms didn't save it: "it is not enough in a legal process to assert that a contract says something without actually providing the contract." (para. 31)
  - The customer "is entitled to be put in the position they would have been in if the misrepresentation had not been made." (para. 33) The order: "$812.02", made up of "$650.88 in damages", "$36.14 in pre-judgment interest" and "$125 in CRT fees" (para. 44).
