# Canada: Vendor False Claims / Straw Men

Each source is quoted exactly from its primary text. Each concerns works with no human author, or a registration or policy question. None of them describes work an author directs with a software tool, an LLM included. The skill's section "Common Vendor-trained Straw-Men / Red Herring Responses" quotes real instances of their misuse. A copy of each source as read is in `sources/`.

- Copyright Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. C-42 (current to 2026-10-19) — read 2026-11-10 (absence) — https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-42/FullText.html (`sources/copyright-act-c42-fulltext.html`) — the Act has no "computer-generated" provision: a search of the full text finds no "computer-generated", and no "artificial intelligence". Misused as: Canadian law has a special rule for work made with AI tools.
- Consultation paper: Consultation on Copyright in the Age of Generative Artificial Intelligence (2023), section 2.2 — read 2026-11-10 — https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/strategic-policy-sector/en/marketplace-framework-policy/consultation-paper-consultation-copyright-age-generative-artificial-intelligence (`sources/ised-2023-genai-consultation-paper.html`) — "A human may contribute sufficient skill and judgment in a work produced with the assistance of AI technologies to be considered the author of the work." Its doubt concerns output made "based solely on short instructions by human users". It is a consultation paper, not law. Misused as: the Government of Canada says work made with an AI tool has no author.
- A Consultation on a Modern Copyright Framework for Artificial Intelligence and the Internet of Things (2021), section 2.2 — read 2026-11-10 — https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/strategic-policy-sector/en/marketplace-framework-policy/copyright-policy/consultation-modern-copyright-framework-artificial-intelligence-and-internet-things-0 (`sources/ised-2021-ai-iot-consultation.html`) — it reports the INDU committee's recommendation that the Act "be adapted to distinguish works made by humans with the help of AI-software from works created by AI without human intervention". The Act has not been so amended (see the first entry). Misused as: Canada has adopted a rule on authorship of AI-assisted work.
- Canadian Intellectual Property Office, A guide to copyright — read 2026-11-10 — https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canadian-intellectual-property-office/en/guide-copyright (`sources/cipo-guide-to-copyright.html`) — "CIPO does not verify or examine the claims made in applications for copyright registration and it cannot guarantee the legitimacy, ownership, authorship or originality of a work." Misused as: a CIPO registration that lists an AI application as an author shows that Canadian law recognizes AI authors.
