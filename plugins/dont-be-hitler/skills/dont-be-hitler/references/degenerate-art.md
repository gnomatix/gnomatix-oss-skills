# "Degenerate art": what the Nazi regime did to art it called objectionable

Background for the `dont-be-hitler` skill. Figures vary between sources because they count different things; ranges are given where they disagree.

## Hitler's position, in his own words

From his speech opening the House of German Art and the first "Great German Art Exhibition", Munich, 18 July 1937 (English translation by GHI staff, *German History in Documents and Images*, from *Völkischer Beobachter*, 19 July 1937; German excerpts in Max Domarus, *Hitler: Reden und Proklamationen 1932–1945*, vol. I, pp. 706–10):

- "the exhibit itself must also bring about a turning away from the degeneracy that we have witnessed in the arts, sculpture, and painting."
- "Cubism, Dadaism, Futurism, Impressionism, and so on, have nothing to do with our German people."
- "I therefore wish to affirm in this hour my immutable resolve to do for German artistic life what I have done in the area of political confusion: to purge it of empty phrases."
- Of the artists: "such conduct would fall within the realm of criminal justice."
- "every person or thing that is participating in this perversion should realize that the hour of his elimination will come sooner or later."

## What followed

- **The purge of German museums (1937–38).** Roughly 16,000 modern works were confiscated from German public collections (estimates run from about 15,550 to almost 21,000). The V&A holds the only known complete inventory of the seizures, listing works from 101 museums.
- **The "Entartete Kunst" exhibition.** It opened in Munich on 19 July 1937, the day after the speech, with some 650 confiscated works from 32 museums, hung to be mocked.
- **The law.** The "Law on the Confiscation of Products of Degenerate Art" of 31 May 1938 made the seizures permanent, without compensation.
- **Sold abroad for foreign currency.** On 30 June 1939, Galerie Fischer auctioned about 125 paintings and sculptures from German museums at the Grand Hotel National in Lucerne. Authorised dealers sold many more.
- **Burned.** On 20 March 1939 (one source gives 30 March), works that were not sold were burned in the courtyard of the Berlin fire brigade headquarters. The reported count is 1,004 paintings and sculptures and 3,825 drawings, watercolours and prints.
- **Private collections looted across Europe (1940–45).** The Einsatzstab Reichsleiter Rosenberg (ERR) seized Jewish-owned collections in occupied France and Belgium. More than 20,000 objects were catalogued at the Jeu de Paume in Paris before being shipped to Germany and Austria. The best went to Hitler's planned Führermuseum in Linz and to Göring's collection; hundreds of Jewish family collections were taken. Heirs are still seeking restitution today.
